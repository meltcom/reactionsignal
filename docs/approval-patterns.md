# Broad approval rules and historical review — October 11, 2026

The latest 20 production discovery/classification approvals (October 7–11 UTC) covered 13 channels. Bulk approvals were counted per video; member submissions were examined separately. The sample included titles without reaction wording and videos stored as Short or Unknown. Unknown format does not establish that duration was missing: it can also represent a video at or below the old three-minute threshold. This is a convenience sample, not a measured false-positive rate.

## Rules

- Apply the same evidence rules to every channel and covered performer; no channel or performer exceptions.
- An exact performer alias in the title can auto-confirm a new public upload under automatic review policy. Reaction wording, a known reactor and duration over three minutes are no longer requirements.
- A title with reaction wording can also qualify when a description sentence explicitly says it reacts to the performer. Tags, original-video references and link lines alone are insufficient.
- Watch parties, tour updates, behind-the-scenes titles and “before … there was …” comparisons remain pending.
- Shorts and missing duration do not block publication. Explicit Short wording with a duration at or below three minutes, or unknown duration, can identify Short format; missing duration otherwise remains Unknown. Moderator format locks take precedence.
- Preserve exclusions, existing match decisions, manual holds, public availability, channel identity checks and performer review policy. Do not restore rejected videos or automatically apply pending previews. Official-channel monitoring remains a separate setting.

## Pitfalls and recovery

Performer-name title matching can publish covers, official performances, news, announcements, clickbait and unrelated uploads using the performer's name. Shorts may be excerpts rather than reactions. Missing duration leaves format uncertain. Description text and titles can be misleading. Context guards reduce obvious cases but cannot recognize every wording. Metadata checks do not watch or verify the video itself.

Moderators can search all catalog statuses in Video Classification Review → Manage catalog approvals, including entries already published or removed. Unapprove returns a video to the approval queue; Hide removes it from every member's catalog; Delete removes it and blocks rediscovery. Each action requires a reason and records moderator, timestamp, and before/after status. Stale or concurrent decisions are rejected. Refreshes and automatic rechecks cannot reverse a moderator hold. Saved metadata, comments and ratings remain intact; Correct / restore approval uses the existing explicit restoration workflow and its audit history. “Delete” is catalog removal, not physical erasure of the retained record or deletion on YouTube.

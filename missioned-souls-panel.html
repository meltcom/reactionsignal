Manual guide: get the title, date, duration, and thumbnail image

Facebook may show a public video in its player without exposing all details to Reaction Journey. Use the steps below for any field left blank.

  1. Copy the video link. Open the Reel or video itself, select Share or the three-dot menu, then Copy link. Paste that direct Reel/video URL above, not the creator's profile. Check that it opens in a private window while signed out.
  2. Enter the title. Use the video's caption or heading shown beside the player. Keep it short enough to identify the clip. The title is required even when Facebook does not provide it automatically.
  3. Find the published date. Look beside the creator's name for the post date. If Facebook shows a relative time such as “2 d”, open the post's date/details to see the full date when available. Enter the date the video was posted, not the day you viewed it. Leave this field blank if Facebook does not show a reliable date.
  4. Find the duration. Start the video and reveal the player controls. Read the total time at the end of the progress bar, then enter the number of seconds only. For example, 00:26 means 26 seconds. Leave it blank if Facebook does not show the total.
  5. Make a thumbnail image file. Pause on a clear frame. On Windows press Win + Shift + S, then click the Snipping Tool notification to open the snip and use Save. On Mac press Shift + Command + 4; the screenshot saves to the desktop. Select just the video frame and save as PNG or JPEG. On a phone, pause the video, take a screenshot, and crop it in Photos/Gallery. Choose the image under Upload permanent thumbnail. Reaction Journey stores an uploaded copy. If you already have a public image URL, paste it into Thumbnail URL; Facebook image URLs can expire, so uploading the image file is more reliable.
  6. If the automatic lookup fails. After checking that the video opens publicly while signed out, choose Continue with manual details. Enter the title and any date or duration you can verify. The date and duration may stay blank when Facebook does not expose them.

Uploaded images are resized and stored by Reaction Journey. Direct Facebook image links can expire.
